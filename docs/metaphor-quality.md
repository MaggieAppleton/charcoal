# Metaphor Explorer quality rubric

Exploration and refinement use `gpt-6.2-sol` with high reasoning effort, independently of the review model. Both receive the source context verbatim. Refinement also receives the selected option and exact feedback. Provider-request tests cover those inputs and the four output fields. Scene quality takes editorial judgement.

- **One relationship.** Pick one action or comparison that carries the source's main idea. Keep enough subjects to make it faithful and clear.
- **A thumbnail read.** Write the shortest faithful scene, usually one short sentence. Picture it without the title. Remove each prop or mechanism the picture can lose and still read at a glance.
- **Three distinct analogies.** Look for everyday situations in which that same relationship is already visible. Give each option a different way to show it.
- **Room to compose.** Describe visible subjects and action in the scene. Use the meaning for the source connection and relevant supporting facts, and the caveat for limitations or misleading implications. Leave art direction open unless requested.
- **A focused revision.** Make the smallest visible change that answers the feedback. Keep accepted elements and comparisons. When asked for a different metaphor, use a new familiar situation that carries the same relationship.

## Illustrative examples

These are editorial examples using synthetic context. They were written by hand.

**Giving a helper only what they need.** The article also discusses audit records and when access expires. The refinement asks to keep the handoff and make it clear that only one item passes through.

| | Scene |
| --- | --- |
| Before | A hand feeds documents through a fortress hatch while one guard checks a clock and another writes in a ledger. |
| After | A hand slips one envelope through the mail slot of a closed door. |

The envelope carries the handoff. The clock and ledger can stay in the article. The meaning can explain that one item passes while the rest stays closed off. The caveat can point out that a mail slot suggests a one-way handoff.

Two other ways to show the same relationship are an eyedropper releasing one drop into a glass (measuring a portion) and a spotlight lighting one shelf of a dark bookcase (revealing a selected part).

**Saving work for one person by giving it to someone else.** The article also discusses deadlines and how teams count completed work.

| | Scene |
| --- | --- |
| Before | On an assembly line, one worker lounges under a clock while another sorts a pile of forms, with arrows tracing how work moves between stations. |
| After | Someone sweeps a pile of leaves onto their neighbour’s lawn. |
